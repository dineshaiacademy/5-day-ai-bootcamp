# Test the app: questions to ask and answers to expect

Once your PDF chat app is running and connected, upload
`../2_Docs/Dinesh_AI_Academy_Policies.pdf`, then try these questions. If the app
is working correctly, its answers should match what's described here.

---

## Positive examples — the answer IS in the PDF

### 1. "What happens if I miss a class?"
**Expect:** You don't lose your spot. Recordings go out within 24 hours of each
session, so you can catch up before the next one. You just need to attend (or
watch the recording of) at least 4 out of the 5 days to still qualify for the
certificate.

### 2. "I need to cancel 3 days before the bootcamp starts — will I get my money back?"
**Expect:** You'll get half your money back (a 50% refund). Cancelling 3 days
before falls in the "6 to 2 days before" window. If you'd cancelled a week or more
in advance, it would've been a full refund; cancelling in the last 48 hours gets
no refund.

### 3. "What do I actually have to do to get the certificate at the end?"
**Expect:** Three things — show up (or watch the recording) for at least 4 of the
5 days, submit the final project on Day 5, and follow the code of conduct
throughout. Miss all three and no certificate.

---

## Negative examples — the answer is NOT in the PDF

This is the most important test of all. It proves the app is actually reading the
document instead of guessing. A well-built app should clearly say the information
isn't covered — not make something up.

### 4. "What's your policy on bringing pets to class?"
**Expect:** The assistant should say the document doesn't mention anything about
pets, rather than inventing a rule that doesn't exist.

### 5. "Who is the founder of Dinesh AI Academy and what's their background?"
**Expect:** The assistant should say this isn't something the policy document
covers — it only has handbook/policy information, not founder bios.

### 6. "If I finish top of my batch, do I get a scholarship for the next bootcamp?"
**Expect:** The assistant should say there's no mention of scholarships or
rewards for top performers in the document, instead of guessing an answer that
sounds plausible.
